Problems first

Attempt these before you open a book or a video. Write the argument in the work box and the conclusion in the answer box. Check counts the problem only when both pass, and it does not open the solution. The solution stays locked for twenty minutes after you start the timer.

Attack 1

Use Gauss’s law to find the electric field of a point charge.

Timer not started. Twenty minutes of real work before a solution.

Spherical symmetry: \(\mathbf E\) is radial and constant in magnitude on a sphere of radius \(r\). Flux is \(E\cdot 4\pi r^2=q/\varepsilon_0\), so \(E=q/(4\pi\varepsilon_0 r^2)\).

Attack 2

Find the field of an infinite line charge with density \(\lambda\).

Timer not started. Twenty minutes of real work before a solution.

Cylindrical symmetry. A Gaussian cylinder of radius \(r\) and length \(L\) has flux \(E\cdot 2\pi r L\) through the curved wall and none through the caps. Enclosed charge \(\lambda L\). Thus \(E=\lambda/(2\pi\varepsilon_0 r)\).

Attack 3

Why is the electric field inside the material of a conductor, in electrostatics, equal to zero?

Timer not started. Twenty minutes of real work before a solution.

If it were not, free charges would accelerate. Electrostatics means they are not accelerating, so the field in the conducting material is zero. The excess charge sits on the surface.

What this actually is

Electromagnetism in this course is Maxwell’s equations in integral and differential form, and the special cases you can compute: electrostatics, magnetostatics, induction, and electromagnetic waves in vacuum. The mathematical content is the divergence theorem and Stokes’ theorem applied to the electric and magnetic fields. Gauss’s law computes the field when the symmetry kills the flux through most of a surface. Ampère’s law does the same for steady currents. Faraday’s law is induction. The Lorentz force is how fields act on charges.

OpenStax Volume 2 is the free working text. Griffiths, Introduction to Electrodynamics, ISBN 978-1-108-42041-9, is the paid book you should buy when you want the subject at the level of a physics major; the ISBN was catalogued and is the standard fourth edition’s number in that catalog entry. MIT 8.02 is the course. Irodov’s electrodynamics problems are the ceiling alongside Griffiths’ harder exercises. Feynman Volume II is the preview, not the drill.

A Gaussian surface that does not match the symmetry is a calculation you invented. State the symmetry before you integrate.

Core results

Statements only. Write the argument before you reveal it. A revealed proof you did not attempt is a paragraph you will not be able to rebuild at 2am.

  1. Electrostatics. \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf E=\rho/\varepsilon_0\), \(\nabla\times\mathbf E=0\) in electrostatics, so \(\mathbf E=-\nabla V\). Gauss’s law in integral form is the flux statement. The potential of a localized charge distribution that vanishes at infinity is the usual Coulomb integral.

    The curl-free claim is Faraday’s law with no changing magnetic field. Existence of the potential is the vector-calculus theorem you proved on the calc-3 page, on a simply connected domain. Gauss’s law is Maxwell’s equation, taken as a law, checked on a point charge by the attack.

  2. Magnetostatics. \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf B=0\). For steady currents, \(\nabla\times\mathbf B=\mu_0\mathbf J\). The Biot–Savart law is the integral solution. The Lorentz force is \(q(\mathbf E+\mathbf v\times\mathbf B)\).

    These are the laws in the steady case. Ampère’s law in integral form follows from Stokes’ theorem. Use it for a wire, a sheet, and a solenoid, and say why the symmetry lets the field come out of the integral.

  3. Induction. Faraday: the circulation of \(\mathbf E\) around a closed loop equals minus the derivative of the magnetic flux through a surface bounded by the loop. Lenz’s law is the minus sign. Motional emf for a rod is the Lorentz force on charges in the rod.

    The flux rule is the law. For a moving rod on rails, computing \(\oint (\mathbf v\times\mathbf B)\cdot d\mathbf l\) reproduces the flux rule. Do that once so the two pictures are the same fact.

  4. Maxwell and waves. The displacement current \(\mu_0\varepsilon_0\partial\mathbf E/\partial t\) repairs Ampère’s law. In vacuum the fields satisfy the wave equation with speed \(1/\sqrt{\mu_0\varepsilon_0}\). A plane wave has \(\mathbf E\), \(\mathbf B\), and the direction of propagation mutually perpendicular, with \(E=cB\) in SI.

    Take the curl of Faraday, use the vacuum Maxwell equations, and the vector identity for \(\nabla\times(\nabla\times\mathbf E)\), together with \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf E=0\). The speed is read off the wave equation. The relation \(E=cB\) is the content of Faraday or Ampère applied to a monochromatic plane wave.

  5. Circuits, as lumped Maxwell. Kirchhoff’s voltage law is Faraday with no changing flux through the circuit. Kirchhoff’s current law is charge conservation. \(RC\), \(RL\), and \(RLC\) transients are linear ODEs.

    Write the ODE for a series RLC from the loop rule and solve it with the methods of the ODE page. The underdamped, critically damped, and overdamped cases are the discriminant of that characteristic polynomial.

Names and links. The problem text stays on the source site. Copyrighted contest problems are not copied here. What follows is where they are and why they hurt.

    Symmetry plus a differential equation. The hard ones are the ones where the Gaussian surface is not obvious.

    They mix a circuit transient with a field problem. You do not get to stay in one chapter.

Mastery gate

Pass: one 8.02 exam, plus written derivations of the point-charge field, the line-charge field, and the vacuum wave speed. Ten Irodov electrodynamics problems. Fail if a Gaussian surface is chosen without a symmetry sentence.

Escalating sets

Five sets. Finish a set before you touch the next. Full sentences, not a circled answer.

Set 1. Electrostatics

  1. Point charge from Gauss.
  2. Line charge.
  3. Infinite sheet, \(E=\sigma/(2\varepsilon_0)\). Derive it.
  4. Field inside a conductor.
  5. Potential of a point charge, and the field as minus its gradient.

Set 2. Magnetostatics

  1. Field of a long wire from Ampère.
  2. Solenoid, interior field \(\mu_0 n I\).
  3. Lorentz force on a charge in a uniform B field: circular motion, radius \(mv/qB\).
  4. Why a velocity selector needs crossed E and B.
  5. \(\nabla\cdot B=0\) implies no magnetic monopoles. Say what the integral form says.

Set 3. Induction

  1. A rod sliding on rails in a uniform B field. Compute the emf two ways.
  2. Lenz’s law, one example where the induced current opposes the change.
  3. Self-inductance definition.
  4. Energy in an inductor, \(\tfrac12 LI^2\), from the power integral.
  5. A transformer, ideal, voltage ratio.

Set 4. Waves and circuits

  1. Derive the wave equation for \(E\) in vacuum.
  2. \(c=1/\sqrt{\mu_0\varepsilon_0}\).
  3. Series RLC, underdamped solution.
  4. RC charging curve.
  5. Displacement current: why Ampère’s law needed a repair. One paragraph.

Set 5. Ceiling

  1. Ten Irodov E&M problems.
  2. One 8.02 exam.
  3. Wave derivation from memory.
  4. Line charge from memory.
  5. Oral Gauss’s law.
