Problems first

Attempt these before you open a book or a video. Write the argument in the work box and the conclusion in the answer box. Check counts the problem only when both pass, and it does not open the solution. The solution stays locked for twenty minutes after you start the timer.

Attack 1

Show that \(y=A\sin(kx-\omega t)\) travels in the positive \(x\) direction with speed \(\omega/k\), if \(\omega\) and \(k\) are positive.

Timer not started. Twenty minutes of real work before a solution.

A crest is where the phase is \(\pi/2\), so \(kx-\omega t=\pi/2\), hence \(x=\omega t/k+\mathrm{const}\). The crest moves at \(\omega/k\) toward increasing \(x\).

Attack 2

Two slits separated by \(d\) are illuminated by wavelength \(\lambda\). Where are the bright fringes on a distant screen?

Timer not started. Twenty minutes of real work before a solution.

The path difference is \(d\sin\theta\). Bright fringes when that is an integer number of wavelengths: \(d\sin\theta=m\lambda\). On a distant screen, \(y\approx L\tan\theta\approx L\sin\theta\) for small angles, so \(y_m\approx m\lambda L/d\).

Attack 3

A standing wave \(2A\sin(kx)\cos(\omega t)\) has nodes where?

Timer not started. Twenty minutes of real work before a solution.

Where \(\sin(kx)=0\), so \(x=n\pi/k\), independent of time. Those points never move.

What this actually is

This page is oscillations that travel. A wave on a string, sound, and light are the same mathematics with different restoring mechanisms: a shape \(f(x-vt)\) that propagates, a linear wave equation, and superposition. Interference is what superposition does when two waves occupy the same place. Optics in this course means geometrical rays as the short-wavelength limit, and then interference and diffraction as the wave phenomena that rays cannot explain: double slit, single slit, and thin films.

OpenStax Volume 3 and MIT 8.03 are the working pair. The course is called vibrations and waves and it is the right level: normal modes, dispersion, and the wave equation, not a museum of lens formulas. Geometrical optics is in OpenStax and you should be able to do a thin lens, but it is not the ceiling. The ceiling is an 8.03 exam and the optics problems in Irodov that require a path difference.

A phase you cannot track is an interference problem you will get backwards. Write the phase before you write the intensity.

Core results

Statements only. Write the argument before you reveal it. A revealed proof you did not attempt is a paragraph you will not be able to rebuild at 2am.

  1. The wave equation. \(u_{tt}=v^2 u_{xx}\) has right- and left-moving solutions \(f(x-vt)\) and \(g(x+vt)\). On a finite string with fixed ends, the normal modes are standing waves with a discrete spectrum.

    The traveling-wave claim is the factoring you did for the PDE page, or a direct chain-rule check. Fixed ends force nodes at the endpoints, hence \(k=n\pi/L\) and frequencies that are integer multiples of the fundamental.

  2. Superposition and beats. The sum of two close frequencies is a rapid oscillation inside a slow envelope. The beat frequency is the difference of the frequencies.

    Use the sum-to-product identity from the precalculus page. The envelope is the slow cosine. Count how often the envelope peaks.

  3. Impedance and reflection. At a boundary, part of a wave reflects and part transmits. A fixed end inverts the pulse. A free end does not. The reflection coefficient is fixed by continuity of the transverse displacement and the balance of transverse forces.

    Write the incoming, reflected, and transmitted waves, match the displacement at the joint, and match the slopes with the tensions. The fixed-end limit is infinite mass on the far side, which forces the reflected amplitude to be minus the incoming amplitude.

  4. Interference. Coherent sources interfere. The phase difference is \(2\pi\) times the path difference over \(\lambda\), plus any reflection phase shift of \(\pi\). Constructive interference when the phase difference is a multiple of \(2\pi\).

    Superpose \(A\cos(\omega t)\) and \(A\cos(\omega t+\phi)\). The amplitude is \(2A\cos(\phi/2)\). Maxima when \(\phi=2\pi m\).

  5. Diffraction and thin lenses. A single slit of width \(a\) has minima at \(a\sin\theta=m\lambda\) for nonzero integers \(m\). A thin lens in the paraxial limit obeys \(1/s+1/s'=1/f\). Ray optics is the limit in which interference fringes are too fine to resolve.

    The single-slit minima come from pairing Huygens sources across the slit so each pair differs by half a wavelength. The lens equation is similar triangles in the paraxial ray diagram, or Fermat’s principle. State which one you are using.

Hardest questions

Names and links. The problem text stays on the source site. Copyrighted contest problems are not copied here. What follows is where they are and why they hurt.

  1. 8.03 exams
    Coupled oscillators and boundary conditions, timed.
  2. Irodov, wave optics
    The geometry of the path difference is the problem. The formula is one line after that.
  3. Derive the double-slit condition
    If you cannot draw the triangle, you will not notice a reflection phase shift later.
  4. Standing waves on a string with one free end
    The spectrum changes. People write \(n\pi\) out of habit.
  5. Beats, from the trig identity, including the beat frequency
    The factor of 2 is the classic mistake. Volume 1 has the oscillation chapter that feeds this.

Mastery gate

Pass: one 8.03 exam, plus written derivations of the traveling-wave speed, the fixed-end normal modes, and the double-slit maxima. Five Irodov optics or wave problems. Fail if a phase shift of \(\pi\) on reflection is inserted without a reason.

Diagrams

Escalating sets

Five sets. Finish a set before you touch the next. Full sentences, not a circled answer.

Set 1. Traveling waves

  1. Speed \(\omega/k\).
  2. Write \(f(x+vt)\) and say which way it moves.
  3. Check that \(f(x-vt)\) solves the wave equation.
  4. Wavelength, period, and \(v=f\lambda\).
  5. A pulse hits a fixed end. Sketch the reflection.

Set 2. Standing waves

  1. Nodes of \(\sin(kx)\cos(\omega t)\).
  2. String of length \(L\), both ends fixed. Frequencies.
  3. One end free. Frequencies.
  4. Beats of 440 Hz and 444 Hz.
  5. Energy in a standing wave, qualitatively: where is it kinetic?

Set 3. Interference

  1. Double slit, the condition.
  2. Path difference for a thin film with one reflection phase shift. State the extra \(\lambda/2\).
  3. Two speakers, constructive interference on the perpendicular bisector. Why?
  4. Intensity proportional to amplitude squared. What happens to intensity when amplitude doubles?
  5. Coherence: why a light bulb and a laser are not the same kind of source.

Set 4. Diffraction and rays

  1. Single-slit minima.
  2. Thin lens equation, one numerical example with signs you explain.
  3. A real image versus a virtual image.
  4. Why geometrical optics fails at a narrow slit.
  5. Resolution, Rayleigh criterion, stated.

Set 5. Ceiling

  1. One 8.03 exam.
  2. Five Irodov wave or optics problems.
  3. Fixed-end spectrum from memory.
  4. Double slit from memory.
  5. Oral explanation of a node.
